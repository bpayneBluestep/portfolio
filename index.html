The story

2019

Director of Growth
Havenwood Academy

The user

I spent three years at , a residential treatment program in Cedar City, Utah, and finished as Director of Growth as part of the leadership team. My job was to increase admissions and decrease churn. We accomplished that through creative online marketing solutions and building a world-class treatment experience. The entire company ran on an EHR called BlueStep, which was my introduction to their product.

BlueStep had tons of gaps that were not fulfilling our needs, but it was highly customizable and built with a proprietary coding language. I taught myself that coding language and began leveraging BlueStep as an instrument to help our company get really efficient.

We built all kinds of new automations, dashboards, auditing tools, and more. Rather than BlueStep being a strain on our operations, we turned it into a center point for delivering care and high quality experiences to patients, their families, and our employees.

2022

VP of Behavioral Healthcare
Xantie Analytics

The builder

The practice I was hired to run was me and one analytics contract. Three months in, that contract ended. I bet on product instead of more consulting.

I took what I learned at Havenwood and joined a data consulting firm called Xantie to broaden my reach. I became excellent at building out impactful dashboards and insights within clinical and financial data for residential treatment centers, and Xantie already had a presence in behavioral healthcare doing this. I joined their team and ran with their behavioral healthcare clients.

When our largest contract was cancelled on us, an insurance billing company, I was left to figure out how we replace that income. This contract alone made up more than 90% of my division's revenue. Rather than leaning into more consulting work, I began building plug and play products and partnered with BlueStep to do it.

The two products I personally designed and shipped began getting traction in the market. I started getting a reputation in the industry as a go to for data and technology consulting. And BlueStep was paying attention.

2025

Executive Director
BlueStep Systems

The executive

The software I started customizing as a user bought the software I built on top of it.

When the executives at BlueStep saw the success we were having with products built on their own infrastructure, they acquired our business and hired me to run the combined companies. I joined as Executive Director, the first dedicated executive operator in the company's twenty year history.

BlueStep had been building every client's system by hand. It kept margins razor thin and turned every new customer into a custom project. Over the next twelve months we rebuilt the product into a modular suite that ships ready to use and gets configured, not coded, for each program. I led that rebuild and wrote about half of its code myself while running the day to day business: pricing, hiring, implementations and the P&L.

Onboarding got faster, per-client engineering dropped, and the ceiling that had capped our growth for years went away.

Now

NATSAP conferences, industry panels, program teams

Sharing what I've learned

The thing I do best is understand a problem well enough to know how software should solve it. More and more, people ask me to explain how.

I present regularly at NATSAP, the national association for therapeutic schools and programs, on technology, design and behavioral healthcare. I get asked to sit on industry panels and to consult with program teams working through their own technology decisions.

The talks change, but the subject rarely does: start with the people and the workflow, and the software gets a lot easier to get right. I've been the user, the builder and the executive, and I still think the user is the one worth listening to.

Marketplace

In design

The referral marketplace

Educational consultants place families with programs, and programs live on those referrals. But everything between the two happens over phone calls and email.

Each side tracks the relationship in its own CRM: who has a bed open, who fits this kid, how the last placement turned out. I'm designing the layer that connects them. The referral becomes a shared record both sides can see and act on, and openings and outcomes flow back so consultants can place families with real evidence.

The hard part is the same as in any two-sided product: each side needs its own view of the same truth, and neither should see what isn't theirs.

What BlueStep is

BlueStep runs the programs where teenagers and young adults live for months while they get treatment. Each one is a hospital, a school, a dorm and a business at once.

BlueStep is the system all of it runs on: the clinical chart, admissions, the school, billing, staffing and the portal parents log into. Every program is different, so every part of it has to be configurable.

A few terms you'll see on this site

Program
A residential treatment center or therapeutic school. Our customers.
Direct-care staff
The people on shift with residents day and night. Rarely at a desk.
Shift note
What staff write about each resident every shift. It becomes part of the medical record.
Milieu
The daily living environment: meals, school, groups, free time.
Educational consultant
An advisor families hire to find the right program. The industry's biggest source of referrals.

What Havenwood Academy is

A long-term residential treatment center in Cedar City, Utah, for young women ages 12 to 18. Students live on campus, go to school on campus and do the work of getting well there.

Havenwood specializes in complex trauma and attachment, and many of its students come from adoptive families. Treatment pairs therapy, including EMDR, neurofeedback and brainspotting, with an accredited school and the everyday life skills that help it hold up after discharge.

It's licensed by the state of Utah, accredited by the Joint Commission and staffed at more than four adults for every student. A sister program for boys, Havenwood SLC, has since opened in Salt Lake City.

Why it matters to this story

A program like Havenwood runs around the clock, and nearly everything it does has to be documented: every shift, every medication, every therapy session, every class. That's a lot of software in the hands of people who would rather be with the kids. It's where I learned what software owes the people who use it.
